**CattoCraft: a little art studio with a resident cat**

**1. Brand direction and design language**

The website should feel like stepping into a small, thoughtfully arranged studio. The cat is its curious host, appearing beside introductions, peeking around illustrations, and curling up in quiet corners.

This theme works whether the products themselves feature cats or include a wider range of handmade art.

| Element | Proposed direction |
|---|---|
| Personality | Warm, curious, slightly mischievous, personal |
| Background | Warm ivory `#FAF6EF`, with subtle paper texture in selected sections |
| Main text | Deep charcoal `#302C29` |
| Primary buttons | Deep terracotta `#873E32` with ivory text |
| Supporting colors | Soft sage `#DCE4D7`, pale peach `#F1DCD0` |
| Typography | A soft serif for headings; a highly readable sans serif for descriptions, navigation, and forms |
| Illustration | One consistent, hand-drawn cat mascot with a small set of poses |
| Photography | Natural light, consistent backgrounds, detail shots, and objects shown at a recognizable scale |
| Shapes | Gently rounded cards, occasional arched image frames, hand-drawn accent lines |
| Motion | Brief, subtle reactions: a blinking cat or a small paw appearing after an item is added |

Keep product photographs visually dominant. Use decoration around content rather than over images or behind important text.

The writing should be mostly clear and descriptive, with a little personality. Navigation, prices, availability, errors, and form labels should remain immediately understandable.

**Tone direction:** Lead ordering notices with personal consultation, tailored quotes, and attentive service. Describe availability, delivery, pricing, and final arrangements as details considered together. Avoid repeating negative payment and reservation disclaimers throughout the shopping journey. The latest design uses production-style **mock confirmations** without preview jargon. Real inbox delivery is a future integration; the mock UI is not evidence that a message was sent.

**2. Site map and core journey**

| Page | Suggested address | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Home | `/` | Introduce the studio and guide visitors into the collection |
| Shop | `/shop` | Browse all available pieces |
| Collection | `/shop/collection-name` | Optional landing page for an established product group |
| Product detail | `/products/product-name` | Show the piece, its story, specifications, and available options |
| Cart & Enquiry | `/enquiry` | Review selections and submit contact details |
| Enquiry confirmation | `/enquiry/thank-you` | Confirm receipt and explain what happens next |

| Supporting policies | Footer links | Explain privacy, delivery, returns, and custom-order terms |

**Main navigation:** Shop · How to order · Say hello · Cart (0). The “Our studio” navigation option has been removed from the header and footer.

Use a recognizable basket icon with a visible “Cart” label and item count. The page itself can carry a warmer heading such as “Your little collection.”

**Primary journey:**

Home or Shop → Product → Add to Cart → Review selections → Send Enquiry → Confirmation → Personal response from the studio

Visitors arriving directly on a product page should understand the enquiry process without first visiting the home page.

“Handmade pieces for cosy corners, thoughtful gifts, and people who notice the little things.”

Primary button: **Explore the collection**

**4. Shop / gallery layout**

Use a regular product grid with generous spacing and consistent image proportions. It should feel curated while remaining easy to compare.

- **Desktop:** Three or four columns, depending on image size.
- **Tablet:** Two or three columns.
- **Mobile:** Two columns where titles remain readable; one column for especially detailed artwork.

Each product card should contain:

- Main product image.
- Clear product name.
- Brief descriptive line.
- Price, “From…” price, or “Price on enquiry.”
- Availability label.
- Link to the product page.

Example:

**The Windowsill Watcher**  
A tiny painted companion for your favourite shelf.  
**Made to order**

Use plain product categories such as **Prints**, **Miniatures**, **Accessories**, or **Originals**, adjusted to the real catalog. Playful collection titles can accompany these labels.

For a small launch catalog, category filters may be enough. Add availability, price, search, and sorting when they help people navigate the actual range.

Useful behavior:

- Preserve filters and scroll position when someone returns from a product page.
- Show active filters and a clear reset action.
- Offer a second image on hover, while keeping all information accessible on touch devices.
- Use “Load more” or pagination for larger catalogs.
- Show sold pieces in a clearly labeled archive if they help demonstrate past work.

**Empty search copy:** “No little treasures found this time. Try another search or clear your filters.”

Avoid quick-add controls at launch if most pieces require customers to choose options or understand customization details.

**5. Product detail page**

On desktop, place the gallery on the left and product information on the right. On mobile, show the main image first, followed closely by the product name, price, availability, and action.

**Recommended content order**

1. Breadcrumb navigation.
2. Product gallery.
3. Product name and short characterful introduction.
4. Price and availability.
5. Required options: size, color, finish, or personalization.
6. Quantity, where applicable.
7. **Add to Cart**.
8. Short enquiry explanation.
9. Full product story and practical details.
10. Related pieces.

Use three to five useful photographs: overall view, close-up, alternate angle, scale reference, and packaging where relevant.

**Copywriting pattern: personality → purpose → facts**

Example for an illustrative product:

**The Windowsill Watcher**

“A pocket-sized professional in the art of doing absolutely nothing.”

“This sleepy little companion brings a quiet bit of mischief to bookshelves, desks, and sunny corners. Each one is finished by hand, with its own tiny quirks and expression.”

Then provide structured facts:

- Materials and finish.
- Exact dimensions.
- What is included.
- Available variations.
- Care instructions.
- Ready-to-ship or made-to-order status.
- Preparation time.
- Customization options and limits.

Playfulness should add character without replacing information someone needs to decide.

**Below Add to Cart:**

“Add this piece to your collection for a personal consultation. We’ll prepare a tailored quote with availability and delivery details, just for you.”

For one-of-a-kind pieces, limit quantity to one. For configurable products, require selections before adding them. Save personalization notes against the specific item.

Use **Ready to ship**, **Made to order**, and **Sold** as the primary availability labels. A secondary line such as “Already found a home” can soften a sold state.

**6. Cart and enquiry experience**

Use a **small cart drawer for quick review** and a **dedicated page for the form**. Both should display the same saved selections.

After adding a product, the drawer should show:

- “Added to your cart.”
- Product image, name, selected options, and quantity.
- **Continue browsing**.
- **Review & Send Enquiry**.

Keep the drawer easy to dismiss. It should support keyboard navigation and return focus to the triggering control when closed.

The full enquiry page should show items on the left and the form on the right on desktop. On mobile, place the item review above the form.

**Item review**

Each item should include its image, linked name, selected options, quantity, price status, and item-specific notes. Allow editing and removal.

If every item has a published price, show an **Estimated item subtotal** and currency. Explain that delivery and any agreed customization are confirmed separately.

If some items require a quote, show the known subtotal plus **“Additional items to be quoted.”** Never represent an unknown price as zero.

**Contact form**

| Field | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Name | Required |
| Email | Required |
| Shipping country | Required |
| City / region | Required where needed for delivery estimates |
| Postal code | Request where needed for delivery estimates |
| Custom notes | Optional |
| Desired arrival date | Optional, explicitly described as a request |
| Phone number | Optional; include country code for a personal call from the team |

Collect a full street address later unless it is genuinely needed to answer the enquiry.

Provide a short privacy explanation and link. Any marketing signup should be separate and optional.

**Final action: Send Enquiry**

Supporting text:

“Begin your personal studio consultation. We’ll review your collection, confirm availability, and prepare a custom quote with delivery and the next steps, all agreed together.”

Publish a response window that the business can consistently meet.

**Submission states**

- While sending: show **Sending enquiry…** and prevent duplicate clicks.
- Validation error: identify the exact field and preserve entered information.
- Connection failure: retain the cart and form so the customer can retry.
- Success: show confirmation only after the enquiry has been saved.
- Changed availability: explain which item changed and let the visitor revise their request.

**Empty cart copy:** “Your basket is enjoying a little catnap. Find a piece you love to get started.”

**7. Confirmation and manual processing**

Suggested confirmation:

**“Your enquiry is safely in our paws.”**

“Thank you—we’ve received your selection. We’ll review availability and delivery to your location, then reply by email.”

Display a reference number, item summary, submitted contact details, and an agreed response window when available. Explain that the team will prepare a personal quote and agree the final order details with the customer. In the current console-only preview, show a preview acknowledgement rather than claiming studio receipt.

The system should save each enquiry in an admin dashboard and send:

- A customer acknowledgement with the selected items.
- A studio notification with contact details, delivery location, item options, quantities, and notes.

Email should be a notification channel; the saved enquiry is the durable record.

**Suggested studio workflow:**

New → Reviewing → Awaiting customer → Confirmed / Closed

After reviewing, the maker replies with availability, final pricing, delivery costs, timing, and payment instructions. Define when a piece becomes reserved and communicate that rule consistently.

For one-of-a-kind work, multiple enquiries must not automatically promise the same piece to multiple people.

**8. Cat-themed UI and supporting pages**

**How to order — approved business model**

1. Choose favourite pieces, add them to a collection, and send an enquiry about the desired items and options.
2. The team follows up personally by email and by phone when a number is supplied, discussing products, current availability, and final pricing.
3. The studio ships globally and regularly supplies large organizations, schools, and hospitals. For quantities above the cart limit, customers add representative pieces and specify the total requested quantity for each in the enquiry notes.
4. Pricing is personally reviewed and adjusted for each enquiry to offer the most affordable rates the studio can, particularly for bulk and organizational orders. Ask for project quantities, destination, timing, and budget; avoid inventing fixed discounts or unapproved guarantees.

The `/how-to-order` page presents these four chapters using studio correspondence, hand-drawn illustrations, large serif headings, and native expandable questions. Keep the page pre-rendered and use CSS Modules for responsive styling and reduced-motion support.

Use one recurring mascot with a small visual vocabulary:

| Location | Cat treatment | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Home hero | Cat beside a studio shelf | Introduces the brand |
| How ordering works | Small paw accents beside numbered steps | Connects the journey visually |
| Add-to-cart feedback | Brief paw or ear movement | Acknowledges the action |
| Empty cart | Sleeping cat in a basket | Makes the empty state friendly |
| Confirmation | Cat beside an envelope | Reinforces receipt |
| Say hello | Cat beside a letter | Welcomes questions and new conversations |

Avoid repeated meowing sounds, moving cursors, or animations that compete with the artwork.

**Studio story:** Keep the studio introduction within the home page rather than a separate navigation destination.

**How to Order / FAQ:** Answer whether an enquiry is a purchase, whether stock is reserved, how delivery is calculated, how customization works, and what happens after submission.

**Say hello (`/contact`):** A standalone general contact form welcomes questions, personal pieces, organizational projects, and introductions. Name, email, topic, and message are required; phone and organization are optional. The organization field appears for larger projects. Direct visitors with selected products to `/enquiry`. Like the current itemized enquiry, submission produces a clearly labeled console-only preview, preserves the note for editing, and keeps contact details out of localStorage. Live delivery can be connected later using the same validated payload.

Across every page, maintain readable contrast, visible keyboard focus, useful image descriptions, comfortable touch targets, and reduced-motion support. Decorative cats should not clutter screen-reader output.

**9. Platform recommendation**
**Revised technical specification**

**1. Recommended stack**

| Layer | Recommendation | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Website | Next.js App Router + React + TypeScript | Product pages, navigation, interactive UI, and enquiry endpoint |
| Styling | CSS Modules + shared CSS variables | Complete control over colors, typography, spacing, and the cat theme |
| Micro-interactions | CSS transitions first; a React animation library only where needed | Responsive buttons, cart transitions, gallery changes, and mascot interactions |
| Catalog | Structured JSON files, validated during builds | Manage 200–300 products without a catalog database |
| Cart | React Context + reducer, persisted in local storage | Store selected products, options, quantities, and item notes |
| Enquiries | One server endpoint forwarding to a managed form inbox | Validate submissions, retain enquiries, and notify the business |
| Images and models | Object storage with CDN delivery | Keep large media files separate from the application |
| Future 3D | Three.js + React Three Fiber, loaded on demand | Custom product viewers and interactive cat scenes |
| Hosting | Managed hosting supporting Next.js and serverless functions | Serve prebuilt pages and run the enquiry endpoint |

**Why Next.js here:** the catalog benefits from prebuilt product pages, page-specific metadata, and a submission endpoint in the same project. Next.js supports generating product routes at build time. [Next.js static generation documentation](https://nextjs.org/docs/app/api-reference/functions/generate-static-params)

Vite + React is a valid alternative for a primarily client-rendered site. It produces a deployable static build, but product prerendering and the enquiry endpoint would need separate arrangements. For this project, Next.js keeps those concerns together. [Vite deployment documentation](https://vite.dev/guide/static-deploy)

Use a normal Next.js deployment with mostly static pages. A pure static export cannot run the proposed POST enquiry endpoint. [Next.js backend documentation](https://nextjs.org/docs/app/guides/backend-for-frontend)

**2. Simple architecture**

The system has three main paths:

- **Browsing:** prebuilt pages → product information → responsive images from the CDN.
- **3D viewing:** visitor selects “Explore in 3D” → viewer code loads → the selected model loads.
- **Enquiry:** browser cart → enquiry endpoint → managed submission inbox → business notification.

No product database, inventory transaction engine, customer account system, or custom admin dashboard is required at launch.

A managed service such as Formspree can provide the submission inbox and notifications. Select a plan with suitable submission volume, retention, and acknowledgement features; stored history is subject to plan limits. [Formspree plans](https://formspree.io/plans/)

**3. Managing 200–300 products**

Use **one structured record per product** and a shared product-page template. Adding an item should mean adding content and assets, rather than designing another page.

Each record should contain:

| Group | Fields |
|---|---|
| Identity | Stable product ID, URL slug, name, category, tags |
| Copy | Short introduction, full description, materials, dimensions, care |
| Ordering | Published price or enquiry-only status, currency, options, availability, lead time |
| Images | Asset URLs, image dimensions, alternative text, display order, focal point |
| Optional 3D | Model URL, poster image, file size, camera settings, accessible description |

Keep high-resolution originals, production images, and model files outside the source repository. Store their references in the catalog.

**Content workflow:**

1. Add or update the product record.
2. Upload its prepared assets.
3. Validate required fields, duplicate IDs, image references, and options.
4. Preview the product.
5. Publish by rebuilding the site.

A spreadsheet-to-JSON import can support bulk updates later. Keep one authoritative source so spreadsheet and website records do not drift.

For search and filtering, generate a compact index containing only IDs, names, categories, tags, prices, availability, and thumbnail references. At this catalog size, simple browser-side filtering is a reasonable starting point.

Do not ship every product’s full description, gallery, and model configuration to the shop page.

**Tradeoff:** availability changes require a content update and deployment. The site should continue to explain that an enquiry does not reserve stock. Introduce a CMS only if editing frequency or nontechnical editing needs justify it.

**4. Image preparation and delivery**

Sharp images come from delivering an appropriate resolution for the displayed size and screen density.

Use an automated asset pipeline:

**Original photograph → resized, compressed variants → versioned CDN files → responsive image component**

Suggested starting widths are **320, 640, 960, 1440, and 2000 pixels**. Adjust these after testing the actual layouts and artwork.

- Generate WebP and/or AVIF, with a suitable fallback.
- Retain archival originals separately.
- Remove unnecessary metadata from delivery files.
- Keep consistent color handling and inspect fine artwork details after compression.
- Store width and height so the browser can reserve space before an image arrives.
- Use `srcset` and accurate `sizes` values so the browser chooses the right file.
- Offer larger detail images only when visitors open zoom or full-screen viewing.

A 300-pixel-wide card on a high-density screen may need roughly a 600-pixel image. Sending the original camera file offers little practical benefit there.

For predictable processing costs, generate image variants during asset preparation and serve them through a small reusable responsive-image component. Alternatively, use `next/image` with a custom loader connected to an image transformation service. Choose one optimization pipeline. [Next.js Image documentation](https://nextjs.org/docs/app/api-reference/components/image)

**Loading order**

| Asset | When to load |
|---|---|
| Main hero or first product image | Immediately; prioritize the principal visible image |
| Other visible product cards | Normally, without competing high-priority requests |
| Images below the fold | Lazy-load as they approach the viewport |
| Gallery thumbnails | Load small versions |
| Large alternate gallery images | When selected, with limited nearby prefetching |
| Full-resolution zoom image | When zoom is requested |
| Hover-only second image | On interaction or selective prefetch |

Do not lazy-load the image responsible for the page’s main visual content. Avoid loading a second photograph for every card simply to support hover effects.

**5. Catalog navigation and rendering**

Use **24 products per page** as the initial design. That gives a 300-item catalog roughly 13 pages.

Recommended behavior:

- Visible pagination with shareable page addresses.
- Filters and sorting represented in the URL.
- Filter changes return to the first results page.
- Returning from a product restores filters and scroll position.
- Pagination reserves consistent layout space.
- Prebuilt default listing pages and product pages support discoverability.

An optional **Load more** button can enhance this later, provided page history and return navigation remain reliable.

Avoid automatic infinite scroll at launch: pagination makes it easier to return to a piece and reach the footer. It also bounds how many image elements remain on the page.

Do not introduce grid virtualization unless measurement shows a problem. A 24-card page generally does not need it.

Control route prefetching as well as image loading. Avoid aggressively prefetching hundreds of product routes, and ensure route prefetching never triggers model downloads.

**6. Future 3D assets**

The catalog should be **image-first, with optional 3D exploration**.

Every model gets a lightweight poster image. Shop cards display that poster or a product photograph. Product pages expose a clear **Explore in 3D** control.

Only after activation should the site import the viewer and request the model. Keeping the viewer unmounted until that point prevents an invisible component from fetching assets early.

For custom cat interactions, use React Three Fiber. For a conventional rotate-and-zoom product viewer, `<model-viewer>` is another option with documented poster and deferred-loading support. Choose the viewer that fits the experience rather than shipping both at launch. [Model Viewer loading examples](https://modelviewer.dev/examples/loading/)

**Model preparation**

- Deliver optimized **GLB/glTF** files.
- Remove hidden geometry, unused materials, and unused animations.
- Reduce polygon counts while checking the visible silhouette.
- Use Meshopt or Draco geometry compression where worthwhile.
- Consider KTX2/Basis textures to reduce GPU texture memory, with the required loader support.
- Start with 1K–2K textures; increase only where close inspection benefits.
- Bake lighting or shadows when real-time effects add little value.
- Reuse materials and limit draw calls.

Tools such as glTF Transform support geometry and texture optimization. Compression settings should be verified against the actual viewer and artwork. [glTF Transform documentation](https://github.com/donmccurdy/glTF-Transform/blob/main/packages/docs/src/lib/pages/cli.md)

**Runtime behavior**

- Allow one active 3D canvas at a time.
- Render on demand when the scene is still.
- Pause animation when off-screen or when the tab is hidden.
- Cap rendering resolution on mobile and reduce effects on slower devices.
- Release obsolete model resources when changing products.
- Keep the poster available if loading fails or WebGL is unavailable.
- Preserve normal page scrolling around touch interactions.
- Honor reduced-motion preferences.

React Three Fiber supports on-demand rendering, which avoids continually rendering an idle scene. [React Three Fiber performance guidance](https://r3f.docs.pmnd.rs/advanced/scaling-performance)

A future 3D cat on the home page should follow the same rules: show its poster immediately, load it after essential content, and fall back gracefully.

**7. Caching strategy**

| Resource | Strategy |
|---|---|
| Versioned images and models | Long browser and CDN caching, typically up to one year with `immutable` |
| Versioned JavaScript and CSS | Long-lived caching managed by the build and host |
| Public HTML | Hosting-managed caching, refreshed or invalidated on deployment |
| Catalog/search data | Versioned with the deployment, or short-lived and revalidated |
| Enquiry responses and personal data | `Cache-Control: no-store`; never shared-cache |

Use filenames containing a content hash or version. A changed image must receive a new URL; otherwise long-lived caches can continue showing the old version.

Configure appropriate content types and cross-origin access for CDN assets. Keep 3D decoders and supporting files versioned too.

Do not add an offline service worker initially. Standard browser and CDN caching cover the main need with fewer stale-content problems.

**8. Lightweight enquiry handling**

The cart stores product IDs, selected options, quantities, and item notes. Persist those selections locally, while keeping contact details out of long-lived browser storage.

When the visitor submits:

1. Send contact details and cart selections to the server endpoint.
2. Validate field lengths, quantities, product IDs, and permitted options.
3. Resolve names and published prices from the deployed catalog.
4. Build a readable itemized enquiry.
5. Forward it to the managed form service.
6. Show confirmation only after the service acknowledges acceptance.

Use server-side spam protection and request limits. Keep credentials on the server. Disable repeat clicks during submission, preserve the form on failure, and handle service limits explicitly. Formspree documents rate-limit errors that the UI must handle. [Formspree system limits](https://help.formspree.io/articles/form-and-project-settings/system-limits)

A request reference helps identify accidental duplicates, but strict retry deduplication requires durable provider support or a small persistent store. Do not promise transaction-style guarantees from browser state alone.

The business should review enquiries in the retained inbox as well as email. Export records according to the chosen retention policy.

**9. UI and development structure**

Build reusable components for product cards, galleries, availability labels, the cart drawer, enquiry fields, and mascot states.

Centralize theme variables for:

- Palette and typography.
- Spacing and corner shapes.
- Illustration stroke styles.
- Animation durations and easing.
- Reduced-motion behavior.

Use transforms and opacity for most micro-interactions. Keep feedback immediate: a cart update should not wait for a decorative animation to finish.

Keep interactivity localized to the components that need it. Product copy and page structure can remain pre-rendered.

For VS Code and Codex collaboration, use a conventional TypeScript project with clearly named components, a documented product schema, a lockfile, an environment-variable example, and standard development, build, lint, and type-check commands. A short project guide should record the theme rules and asset budgets.

**10. Performance targets and verification**

Treat these as initial engineering budgets, to be adjusted after reviewing real artwork:

| Measure | Starting target |
|---|---|
| Products initially rendered | 24 per listing page |
| Card image transfer | Approximately 40–120 KB each |
| Main product image transfer | Approximately 150–400 KB |
| Initial viewport image transfer | Aim below 1 MB on mobile |
| 3D transfer before activation | Zero |
| Typical optimized product model | Aim for 1–5 MB |
| Models above 10 MB | Require review or a lighter alternative |
| Simultaneously active 3D viewers | One |

File size alone is insufficient for 3D: also measure decode time, texture memory, draw calls, and responsiveness.

Before launch, test a full 300-item catalog on a midrange phone with a throttled connection. Verify image sharpness, layout stability, cart responsiveness, return navigation, failed submissions, and model fallback behavior.

Confirm through the browser’s network tools that opening the shop does not download the entire catalog’s images or any 3D models.

**10. Data, reliability, and launch scope**

Each **product** should store its ID, name, address, description, images, category, materials, dimensions, variations, price status, currency, availability, lead time, and customization rules.

Each **cart item** should retain the product ID, selected variation, quantity, and item notes. Preserve the cart across refreshes without requiring an account. Keep customer contact information out of long-lived browser storage.

Each **enquiry** should save a reference number, timestamp, customer details, shipping location, item details as submitted, displayed prices, notes, processing status, and notification status.

Before accepting an enquiry, the server should validate products, options, quantities, and current prices. Add spam protection and duplicate-submission protection. Save the enquiry before sending notifications, and retry failed email delivery without creating another enquiry.

**Launch essentials**

- Cohesive visual system and reusable page components.
- Home, shop, product, enquiry, confirmation, and supporting pages.
- Product editing and availability management.
- Persistent cart with options and item notes.
- Saved enquiries and reliable notifications.
- Responsive layouts, accessibility, and optimized photography.
- Search-friendly product titles, descriptions, and share images.
- Basic measurement of product views, cart additions, enquiry starts, and successful submissions.

**Later additions**

Wishlists, customer accounts, advanced filtering, back-in-stock notifications, commission uploads, and online payments can follow when customer behavior justifies them.

**Launch acceptance criteria**

A customer must be able to browse on a phone, choose product options, refresh without losing selections, edit the cart, send an enquiry, and receive an accurate acknowledgement. The studio must receive the same itemized details, recover enquiries when email fails, and clearly distinguish requests from confirmed purchases.

**Current implementation: materials, languages, and launch preparation**

- The active catalog contains **67 pieces**. Product IDs and image mappings remain stable; removed entries are not automatically restored from asset folders.
- Material details use **Artisan Casting Plaster / Gypsum Composite**. Making notes distinguish the solvent-free, non-toxic casting mix from individually confirmed paints and finishes, describe room-temperature casting, and explain practical reuse of offcuts.
- Dimensions are **approximate miniature guides around 5 × 3 × 3 inches**, varied by piece and clearly labeled indicative. They are not measured manufacturing specifications. Final dimensions are confirmed with an enquiry, alongside the invitation: **“Tell us about the spot you have in mind.”**
- English URLs stay unchanged; Italian uses **`/it`**. English pages have concrete routes in **`app/(english)`**; Italian pages use **`app/[locale]`** with the generated `it` locale. Both reuse the studio shell and localized page components, and English links no longer depend on internal rewrites. **`data/products.it.json`** supplies Italian product stories keyed by stable IDs. The flag dropdown offers **English** and **Italiano**, preserves the current route and filters, and the same saved cart works in both languages. Existing browser cart keys remain stable across the CattoCraft rebrand.
- Versioned product images use long-lived caching. The catalog stays paginated at 24 cards; large zoom images are mounted on inspection.
- **Contact and enquiry forms remain mock submissions.** They show finished-looking confirmations; data stays in browser memory, with development-only console logging. Real inbox delivery is required before accepting customer enquiries. Search indexing defaults to disabled during review.
